## What actually auto-rejects

Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, and Taleo reject early-career applications with form rules, not by scoring prose. A required question about visa sponsorship, answered the way the requisition forbids, moves the profile to rejected or archived before a recruiter opens it. Security groups and keyword rankers are a different step. The first cut is the boolean on the form.

For someone on F-1 OPT the combined question ("now or in the future") is the trap. "No" is accurate for work authorized today and false if an H-1B will be required later. "Yes" trips requisitions that have sponsorship turned off.

## What changed after 2023

The 2021–2023 market hired for capacity and would train. The 2025–2026 market wants a stack match and immediate output, with less appetite for sponsorship and a surplus of people with more years than the req asks for. A new grad is often in the same pile as engineers with several years of commercial work.

Recruiters scan a resume for a few seconds looking for the strings in the job description. A strong low-level project that never says the stack on the req reads as a mismatch, including to an ATS skill filter that is just an AND of those strings.

## Why a strong resume still gets no screen

Four separate filters, any one of which is enough:

1. The sponsorship question auto-rejects before a human looks.
2. More-experienced applicants are sorted above a recent graduate for the same req.
3. A short contract, unlabeled, looks like a performance exit.
4. Niche project language hides ordinary backend readiness (API, database, cloud, containers).
