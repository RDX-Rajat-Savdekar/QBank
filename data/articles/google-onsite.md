## The building

You badge in. The lobby is quieter than you expected. You sit with a sticker and a water bottle and try not to rehearse the first sentence of your intro out loud.

The interview rooms are smaller than the videos. Whiteboard or laptop is a coin flip. Ask in the first thirty seconds so you are not mid-problem when they slide a laptop over.

## What they were actually grading

Not “did you finish Two Sum.” More like:

- Did you restate the problem in your own words before touching code?
- Did you pick a representation and say why?
- When you got stuck, did you talk, or did you go silent?
- Would they want you as a teammate for the next forty-five minutes?

Googleyness showed up as follow-ups, not as a separate “values” slide. How you treated a vague constraint. Whether you asked who the user was.

## Coding rounds

Two coding. One was a graph that looked like a grid. One was an iterator / stream problem. Both had a follow-up that invalidated the first data structure.

What helped: writing the brute force in two minutes, then saying the bottleneck out loud, then switching. What did not help: jumping to the clever solution and then failing a case you never wrote down.

## The system-design-ish round

Not a full HLD. More “design the API and talk through scale.” They wanted interfaces and failure modes, not boxes for the sake of boxes.
